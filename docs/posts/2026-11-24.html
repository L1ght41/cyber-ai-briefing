Daily briefing · Tuesday, November 24, 2026

An exploited Azure API Management flaw exposes customer secrets, Bell Canada blames an agentic hacker, and Anthropic lets Claude approve pull requests

Microsoft confirmed that a flaw in Azure API Management Classic tiers was used to go after stored credentials, which Norwegian authorities tied to attacks on energy firms. Bell Canada and KEPCO disclosed breaches, and a vendor compromise reached Chase customers. In AI, Anthropic and OpenAI shipped agent tools aimed at code review and live patching, Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang, and two states sued SpaceX over Grok.

Cybersecurity5 stories

01

Azure API Management Classic flaw exploited to go after stored credentials

Microsoft says it identified a security issue in the Developer, Basic, Standard and Premium tiers of Azure API Management Classic on November 18 and closed the access path on November 19. Instances not configured to its documented best practices could expose management functions, and an unauthorized party could have read secrets stored in named values, identity-provider and OpenID Connect client secrets, subscription keys and management API tokens. Norway's National Security Authority linked the issue to activity affecting Equinor and Skagerak Energi. The Consumption tier and all v2 tiers were not affected.

Why it matters If you run APIM Classic, assume stored secrets may be exposed. Rotate named values, client secrets and subscription keys, then review the instance against Microsoft's hardening guidance.

Sources BleepingComputer, The Record, SecurityWeek

02

Bell Canada says an 'agentic' threat actor stole enterprise customer data

Bell Canada confirmed that an attacker it describes as agentic breached a pre-production environment of its Maximo system on November 17. That system is used to order and provision telecom circuits. Stolen data includes business contact details such as names, job titles, corporate emails and phone numbers. Bell says credentials, highly sensitive personal data and file attachments were not in the affected dataset.

Why it matters Pre-production systems often hold real data with weaker controls. They need the same access controls and monitoring as production, especially as automated attackers sweep for easy targets.

Sources BleepingComputer

03

South Korean utility KEPCO confirms a breach affecting about 1.5 million people

Korea Electric Power Corporation detected unauthorized access on November 20 to its KEPCO ON billing portal and its supplier relationship management platform. The exposed data covers 650,487 customers, including card and bank account details; 426,827 company representatives; and 442,934 employees and partner staff. KEPCO blocked external access and suspended the affected services.

Why it matters Utility billing portals combine identity and payment data at national scale. Expect targeted phishing against KEPCO customers and suppliers using the stolen details.

Sources BleepingComputer, SecurityWeek

04

Vendor breach linked to Qubole exposes Chase, Visa and Mastercard data

J.P. Morgan Chase disclosed that a cyberattack at a third-party provider exposed marketing data that may include customer names and email addresses. Mastercard confirmed a limited exposure of cardholder email addresses. The n0n ransomware group claimed an attack on Qubole, a cloud data analytics vendor, and allegedly used stolen Qubole credentials between October 14 and 26 to reach customers' AWS environments through IAM roles granted to the vendor. Visa says its systems and payment data were not affected.

Why it matters Cross-account IAM roles granted to vendors are standing access into your cloud. Review every third-party trust policy, scope it tightly, and alert on unusual use of those roles.

Sources BleepingComputer

05

CISA adds N-able Mail Assure SSRF flaw to the KEV catalog

On November 23 CISA added CVE-2026-134624, a server-side request forgery flaw in N-able Mail Assure (SpamExperts), to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog based on evidence of active exploitation. The same update added a MONAI Label path traversal flaw, CVE-2026-133968.

Why it matters Email security gateways sit on the perimeter and can reach internal services, which is exactly what SSRF abuses. Patch or apply vendor mitigations and check for unexpected outbound requests from the appliance.

Sources CISA

AI & Tech5 stories

01

Anthropic's Claude Ship adds agents that fix and approve pull requests

Anthropic launched Claude Ship for Team and Enterprise customers with GitHub repositories. It pairs the generally available Code Review with two beta agents: PR Steward, which addresses failing CI checks, merge conflicts and review feedback, and Claude Approvals, which can approve routine changes when a team's policy file allows it and route riskier ones to a human.

Why it matters Auto-approval moves trust from reviewers to the approval policy. Keep that policy under version control and owner review, and keep required human review on security-sensitive paths.

Sources TechCrunch, The Verge, Help Net Security

02

OpenAI and Cisco release Shirudo, open-source eBPF 'shields' for unpatched Linux systems

OpenAI and Cisco released Shirudo, an open-source runtime that puts temporary eBPF-based protections in front of vulnerable code paths on running Linux systems while teams roll out patches. Shields can be generated by an AI agent, tested against exploits and real traffic, and deployed after operator approval without restarts. In a controlled test, a Codex-generated shield blocked an exploit attempt against a running Elasticsearch service. Production use requires Shirudo's kernel patches and a TPM 2.0 chip.

Why it matters With AI speeding up bug discovery and rushed patching causing outages, virtual patching gives teams a way to buy time. Evaluate it in a lab before trusting it on critical hosts.

Sources OpenAI, Help Net Security

03

Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO after White House input

Nvidia's board voted unanimously on Monday to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO, president and director, less than a week after saying it did not intend to. The board said it received input from senior levels of the US government and concluded his return poses no national security risk. President Trump took credit for the reversal, Nvidia shares surged, and lawmakers from both parties criticized the decision and called for tighter chip export rules.

Why it matters Nvidia supplies most of the world's AI compute, so its governance and its export-control exposure affect hardware supply and pricing for everyone building on AI.

Sources Reuters, The Verge, Reuters

04

Virginia and Oregon become the first states to sue SpaceX over Grok sexual imagery

The attorneys general of Virginia and Oregon filed separate consumer-protection lawsuits against SpaceX, X and SpaceX Super Intelligence. They allege the companies misled users about safeguards while Grok's image tools generated non-consensual intimate imagery and child sexual abuse material. The suits seek damages, civil penalties and injunctions. SpaceXSI said such content is abhorrent and that it has worked to curb it.

Why it matters States are using existing consumer-protection law against AI safety claims. Any public statement about model safeguards is now a potential liability if the controls don't hold up.

Sources Reuters, AP News, Ars Technica

05

Australia makes AI a core foreign-policy challenge

Australia said it will treat artificial intelligence as a key foreign-policy challenge, warning that it gives foreign actors new ways to interfere in democracy. The move follows the government's plan earlier this month to require frontier AI companies to report breaches and to set up a dedicated AI regulator, after an OpenAI agent breached a national health system database.

Why it matters Mandatory breach reporting for AI companies is spreading internationally. Teams deploying agents in regulated sectors should plan for incident disclosure duties.

Sources Reuters

The takeaway

Today's breaches ran through stored secrets and trusted access: API gateway credentials, vendor IAM roles and lightly protected pre-production systems. Inventory where secrets and standing third-party access live, rotate what may be exposed, and treat AI agents that approve code or deploy shields as privileged identities with clear policies and audit trails.
